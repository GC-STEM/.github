# Code of Conduct

GC-STEM repositories support teaching, learning, and collaboration.
Students, faculty, staff, contributors, and visitors are expected
to help maintain a respectful and inclusive community.

People bring different experiences, languages, abilities, and
levels of technical knowledge. Questions and mistakes are a normal
part of learning.

## Respectful Participation

- Treat others with courtesy and respect.
- Focus feedback on ideas, code, and processes.
- Explain suggestions clearly and give others room to ask questions.
- Ask for clarification when a message's meaning or tone is unclear.
- Respect personal boundaries and requests to end an interaction.
- Credit other people's work and contributions.
- Allow time for replies. Participants work at different times and
  balance learning with other responsibilities.

Disagreement is welcome when expressed respectfully. Harassment,
discrimination, threats, personal attacks, unwanted sexual conduct,
spam, impersonation, and retaliation are unacceptable.

## Academic Integrity

Follow the course's rules for collaboration, reused material, and
AI assistance. Cite sources and disclose assistance when required.

Do not misrepresent authorship or share restricted assessment
materials. Share assignment solutions only when course instructions
permit it.

If you are unsure what assistance or sharing is allowed, ask your
instructor privately. Do not investigate or debate suspected
academic-integrity violations in public GitHub conversations.

## Privacy and Security

Protect other people's personal, academic, and account information.

Before posting files, screenshots, or logs, remove student records,
grades, private feedback, personal contact details, private
communications, passwords, and other credentials.

Do not conduct security testing without authorization or use code
to harm others. Authorized instructional security activities must
remain within the assignment's permitted scope.

If you encounter exposed information, follow
[private security reporting](https://github.com/GC-STEM/.github/blob/main/SECURITY.md).

## Where This Applies

This code applies to repository-related issues, discussions, pull
requests, reviews, commits, wikis, and associated collaboration
spaces. It also applies when someone officially represents the
repository community.

SNHU policies, course requirements, and GitHub's terms also apply.

## Report a Concern Privately

Do not post sensitive conduct reports in a public issue or discussion.

- **Students:** Contact your instructor using the private method
  listed in Brightspace.
- **If the concern involves your instructor, or you are uncomfortable
  reporting to them:** Contact your academic advisor or the SNHU
  reporting office identified in your student policies.
- **Other participants:** Use the institutional or repository contact
  identified in the affected repository's README.
- **GitHub policy violations:** Use [GitHub Support](https://support.github.com/).

Describe what happened, where and when it occurred, and any relevant
links. Share sensitive evidence only through an appropriate private
channel.

Reports should be handled with care for privacy, consistent with
institutional procedures and any required investigation or referral.

## Moderation and Review

Maintainers are the people responsible for managing a repository.
They may clarify expectations, request corrections, remove content,
limit conversations, restrict participation, or refer concerns to
the appropriate institutional process.

Responses should consider the circumstances, severity, impact,
and any repeated behavior. Serious concerns may require immediate
action.

If a restriction affects access to required coursework, maintainers
should coordinate with the responsible course owner to identify an
approved way to continue the course.

Academic or institutional disciplinary decisions follow the
applicable institutional process. Repository moderation does not
replace that process.
